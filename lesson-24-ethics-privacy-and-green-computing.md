## Level: B2–C1
## Time: 90–120 minutes

### 1. Lesson Objectives / اهداف درس

- واژگان اخلاق کاربردی (نیکی‌رسانی، ضررنرسانی، خودمختاری، رضایت آگاهانه) را در یک تصمیم واقعی به‌کار ببرید.
- اصول حریم خصوصی داده (کمینه‌سازی، محدودیت هدف، مبنای قانونی) را توضیح دهید و یک اطلاعیهٔ حریم خصوصی را نقد کنید.
- عدالت الگوریتمی را تحلیل کنید و نقش «متغیر نیابتی» را با یک مثال روشن کنید.
- هزینه‌های زیست‌محیطی یک سرویس را در دو دستهٔ کربن عملیاتی و کربن نهفته جدا کنید.
- شاخص‌های بهره‌وری انرژی (PUE، انرژی بر عملیات، توان بی‌کاری) را بخوانید و یک گلوگاه را تحلیل کنید.
- با ربط‌دهنده‌ها و لحن محتاطانه بنویسید و ادعای مطلق را به ادعای سنجیده تبدیل کنید.

### 3. Reading / متن اصلی

**عنوان متن: The Cost Behind the Screen (هزینه‌ای که پشت صفحه پنهان است)**

**[1]** Two subjects that sound soft are actually measured in numbers. Ethics asks who is helped and who is harmed by a system, and green computing asks what the system costs in energy, water, and materials. Roya is a data protection officer at a university health centre, and Saman runs the cloud platform of a mid-sized company. Neither of them writes much code, and both of them change what is built, because they ask for evidence before a decision, not after it.

**[2]** Moral questions in computing rarely arrive as a dilemma with two clean options. More often, a principle such as fairness conflicts with a duty to a patient, or an obligation to a customer conflicts with stewardship of a shared resource. A precautionary approach asks a team to act before the evidence is complete, and the precautionary principle is the formal version of that habit. Morality is the older word for the same subject, and it is used here because a decision can be perfectly legal and still wrong. What makes a decision defensible is not the choice itself but the reasoning that is written down, and this is why a review meeting with minutes is more useful than a clever argument in a corridor.

**[3]** The classical vocabulary is still the clearest. Beneficence asks a team to produce a real benefit, non-maleficence asks it not to cause harm, and autonomy asks it to leave the decision with the person who is affected. Paternalism is the failure of the third: a system that decides for a user because the designers believed they knew better. Informed consent is the instrument that protects autonomy, yet it tends to fail in exactly the cases that matter, because a vulnerable group — a patient with a cognitive impairment, a student under pressure, an elderly user — may not be able to read a long form carefully. In some settings, therefore, consent must be obtained from a representative as well as from the person.

**[4]** Every project has stakeholders, and a list of them is longer than a sponsor list. A public interest test may add a ministry, a group of patients, or the staff who will operate the system. A conflict of interest must be declared rather than avoided, because a team that sells a tool cannot be the only reviewer of its accuracy. Dual use is the harder case: a model that predicts disease progression may also be used by an insurer, and a research dataset may be reused in a way that nobody intended. Responsible innovation therefore means writing the second use down before the first version is shipped.

**[5]** When something does go wrong, a chain of words is waiting: liability, negligence, compensation, remedy, and redress. None of them is a technical term, and all of them are measurable in a budget. A whistleblower is the person who reports a problem from inside, and the protection offered to that person is often the best signal of an organisation's real values. In a university, the ethics review board, or institutional review board, is the gatekeeper for research that involves people, and a competent board asks the same question every time: what would happen if this went wrong, and who would notice?

**[6]** The practical instruments are modest. A code of conduct names the behaviour that is expected, due diligence checks a supplier before a contract, and an impact report describes what changed after a system was deployed. A governance board with the power to stop a project is worth more than ten pages of principles, because power, rather than rhetoric, decides difficult cases.

**[7]** Privacy begins with restraint rather than with a lock. Data minimisation is the rule that you collect the least that the task requires, and purpose limitation is the rule that the data may not be reused for a different purpose without a new justification. A legal basis is what makes processing lawful, and a legitimate interest is the weakest of those bases, so it must be documented rather than assumed. A privacy notice must describe the practice that actually exists, and a consent flow that offers no real choice is weaker than no consent at all. An opt-in is a genuine choice; notice and choice as a slogan has been criticised precisely because a user cannot choose what they cannot understand.

**[8]** Rights have names because they can be exercised. A data subject may ask for a copy of the record, request erasure, or demand portability of the data in a usable format. The right to be forgotten is the most quoted and the least absolute of them, because a legal obligation or a public interest may outweigh it. A data protection officer is the person who receives those requests, an automated decision with no human in the loop must be disclosed, and profiling must be described in plain terms rather than hidden in a technical annex.

**[9]** A quiet industry sits behind the screen. A data broker buys and sells profiles, targeted advertising pays for much of the free web, and profiling models decide which advertisement is worth a tenth of a cent. Meanwhile, most organisations keep dark data that nobody reads, data hoarding that grows without a decision, and cold storage that preserves files for no purpose. Deduplication removes the same file twice, data disposal and secure destruction are the planned end of a record, and the most expensive data in any system is the data that nobody can name.

**[10]** The digital divide is the oldest unfairness in the field, and digital literacy is its companion. A service that is technically accessible can still be unusable for a user who has no device, a slow connection, or a language that the interface does not support. Digital rights are the legal list of what a user may ask for, and inclusive design is the technical answer: a service that is built with assistive technology in mind also serves a user with a slow connection or a temporary injury. Plain language is therefore a technical requirement rather than a courtesy, and a form that a student cannot read is a form that produces inaccurate data.

**[11]** Fairness is not the same as equality. Equity asks whether two groups with different starting points can end up with similar outcomes, and a system may be neutral in its code and unequal in its effects. Inclusion is the practical version of the same idea: the group that is affected is present in the room where the decision is made. Representation in a training set decides who is recognised; underrepresentation of one group produces a model that quietly fails for that group, and exclusion from a service is often invisible until somebody complains. A stereotype enters through data, discrimination appears in a decision, and redlining is the historical example of a rule that uses a proxy for a protected group. A proxy metric is the modern version: a variable such as cost, postcode, or school, which correlates with a protected attribute and silently replaces it.

**[12]** Not every failure has an author. An unintended consequence is the normal result of a change in a complex system, a side effect is what it produces somewhere else, and a trade off is the choice that a designer makes with the knowledge available at the time. An opportunity cost is the option that was not taken, an externality is a cost that lands on somebody who did not choose it, and the tragedy of the commons explains why individual rational decisions can exhaust a shared resource. Collective action is the answer, and it is slow by design.

**[13]** Attention is the resource that a digital product sells, and manipulation is the quiet version of that trade. Persuasive design nudges a user towards a longer session; a dark pattern makes the exit harder than the entrance. The attention economy rewards this design, screen time grows, and notification fatigue produces a user who reads nothing. Digital wellbeing is the response, and it is often implemented as a setting that the product itself does not promote.

**[14]** The information environment is part of the same system. Polarisation is measured in surveys rather than in feelings, a filter bubble and an echo chamber describe the way that recommendations narrow what a user sees, and content moderation is the process that decides what stays. A community standard is the written rule, and a user report is the first signal, but a rule that is not published cannot be trusted and a system that removes everything is not moderation either.

**[15]** Minors need a separate paragraph. Age verification is hard to do without collecting more data than the service needs, parental control is easy to install and easy to bypass, and child safety is a requirement that no reasonable designer treats as optional. There is no clean solution here, only a documented balance, and a team that claims a perfect solution is probably selling one.

**[16]** Green computing starts with the same discipline. Sustainable practice asks what a system consumes across its whole life, and carbon accounting converts that consumption into emissions of carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gas. A number for a whole company is nearly meaningless; a number for one workload, one machine, or one job is actionable.

**[17]** Two kinds of carbon must be separated. Operational carbon is produced while a machine is running, and embodied carbon was spent to build it, ship it, and eventually dispose of it. Embodied energy is the same idea in energy terms, and it is large enough that keeping a laptop for two extra years can outweigh a more efficient replacement. Carbon intensity measures how clean the electricity is, and it changes by the hour: the energy mix of a grid, and therefore the grid intensity of one hour, may be twice the average of the day.

**[18]** Efficiency has its own vocabulary. Power usage effectiveness divides the total energy of a data centre by the energy of the computing equipment, so a value of 1.5 means that roughly one third of the energy went to cooling and distribution. Energy proportionality is the ideal in which a machine uses power in proportion to the work it does, and idle power is the reality that breaks it: a server that does nothing may still draw half of its full power, and this unusable power draw is the first thing that a dashboard should expose. Energy per operation and operation per joule are the honest metrics, because they compare two designs on the same work.

**[19]** Water and heat complete the picture. Water usage is large in cooling towers, a water footprint counts the litres that a service consumes indirectly, and a data centre that recovers its waste heat can send it to a nearby building. None of this is free; each of the three choices adds cost, complexity, or both, and each one is justified only by a measured number.

**[20]** The hardware life cycle is where most of the carbon hides. Electronic waste is the fastest-growing waste stream in many countries, and only a small share of it is collected formally. Recycling recovers materials, refurbishing extends the life of a device, and reuse moves it to a second user. Right to repair is a legal answer to planned obsolescence, and repairability, upgradability, and longevity are the design properties behind it. A circular economy and a take-back programme are the business models that make the loop real rather than theoretical.

**[21]** Software has a footprint too. Bloatware and software bloat consume resources without producing value, frugal computing treats efficiency as a feature, and a data diet is a habit rather than a product. On the infrastructure side, deduplication removes duplicate copies, cold storage moves an unused file to a cheaper medium, and a sensible default setting — a lower video quality, a dark mode, an off switch for autoplay — saves energy at a scale that no individual user could reach alone.

**[22]** Procurement is the strongest lever that a public organisation has. A green procurement rule can require an energy label, an efficiency standard, and a supplier code, and a market incentive of that size changes a supply chain faster than any campaign. A conflict mineral clause and a supply chain audit put a labour practice inside the contract, and this is why procurement is the least glamorous and most effective instrument in the chapter.

**[23]** Reporting closes the loop, and it is where greenwashing appears. A sustainability report and a carbon disclosure are useful only if the same boundary is used from year to year; a science-based target and a net zero promise mean little without a baseline. Offsetting and a carbon offset are legitimate instruments, provided that the emission was reduced first and the certificate is verified. In contrast, a vague green claim is cheap to publish and expensive to defend, and regulators in several regions have begun to say so.

**[24]** Roya's first task was a review of a triage model that a hospital group wanted to donate to the health centre. The vendor's report claimed that the model was accurate, and it was accurate on average. Her question was different: for which patient group, at which threshold, and according to which definition of a true positive.

**[25]** The answer took three weeks to obtain. Performance was broadly similar for two groups, but the model's proxy metric — the previous cost of care — underestimated the needs of patients who had historically received less treatment. Consequently, the same score meant a different risk for two groups of people. It is possible that the effect would have been small in a different clinic, and it is also possible that the vendor simply had not looked.

**[26]** Her recommendation was not a refusal. The centre accepted the tool with three conditions: a documented proxy audit, a human review of every high-risk case, and a privacy notice written in plain language. She also asked for a data minimisation review, because the vendor had requested ten years of history for a task that needed two. Moreover, she insisted that the agreement describe the second use of the data, since the same dataset could be valuable to an insurer.

**[27]** Saman's project began with a bill. His company's cloud spending had grown by a third in a year, and the finance director wanted an explanation rather than a discount. He built a dashboard that joined three numbers: the energy per operation of each service, the grid intensity of the region in which it ran, and the utilisation of each resource.

**[28]** The first result was uncomfortable. A large share of the bill came from idle power that nobody had requested: development machines that were never switched off, and a cluster that had been overprovisioned for a launch that was postponed. The second result was subtler. Some batch jobs ran at night because that was the habit, and the grid intensity at night in that region was, on average, higher than in the afternoon, when solar generation peaked.

**[29]** The changes were small and cumulative. Load shifting moved flexible jobs into the cleaner hours, rightsizing reduced the size of the cluster, and a retention rule deleted a class of log files that no one had read in two years. Consequently, the bill fell by roughly a fifth, and the reported emissions fell by a little more, because the remaining work ran in a cleaner hour. None of this required a new technology; it required a measurement and a decision.

**[30]** What the two of them share is a habit of evidence. Both of them separate what is measured from what is assumed, both of them write down the boundary of their claim, and both of them know that a number can be accurate and still misleading. In short, an ethics review and a carbon dashboard are the same instrument in two costumes: a way of asking what the system does to people, and to the world, before somebody else discovers it.

**[31]** So what should a student take from this final chapter? Learn the vocabulary, because a privacy right that cannot be named cannot be exercised. Learn to hedge properly, because a claim that is too strong will be refuted by the first counter-example. Learn to measure one workload, one group, or one kilogram of carbon, because a number for everything is a number for nothing. Finally, learn to write the decision down, since the reasoning is the part that survives a change of staff.

**[32]** A short list of measurements is worth keeping. For fairness: the performance of each group at the chosen threshold, and the proxy variable that stands in for a protected attribute. For privacy: the number of fields that were collected, the number that were used, and the number that were deleted. For the environment: the energy per operation, the carbon intensity of the hour, the utilisation of the resource, and the lifetime of the device. Each of these numbers is unglamorous, and each of them is actionable.

**[33]** It would be convenient to end with a rule, and it would also be dishonest, because every rule in this chapter has an exception that a careful reader can find. What can be said with confidence is narrower and more useful: a system that measures its effects, documents its boundary, and keeps a person in the decision is more likely to be trusted than a system that promises more. To conclude, the screen is not free, and neither is the decision behind it — however, both can be examined, provided that somebody is willing to look.

**ب) نکات ترجمه**

۱. **ربط‌دهنده را با معنای تضاد یا نتیجه ترجمه کنید:** *however* ← «با این حال»، *nevertheless* ← «با این وجود»، *moreover* ← «علاوه بر این»، *consequently* ← «در نتیجهٔ آن»، *whereas* ← «در حالی که».
۲. **ترتیب تسلیم را در فارسی حفظ کنید:** *Although the tool was accurate, it was unequal in its effects* ← «هرچند ابزار دقیق بود، در اثرگذاری نابرابر بود»؛ آوردن «اما» بعد از «هرچند» خطای رایج است.
۳. **لحن محتاطانه را با «به‌نظر می‌رسد» برگردانید:** *The model appears to underperform for one group* ← «به‌نظر می‌رسد مدل برای یک گروه ضعیف‌تر عمل می‌کند». به‌جای «معلوم است که» که ادعایی مطلق می‌سازد.
۴. **منبع را نام ببرید:** *According to a 2024 United Nations report, ...* ← «بر پایهٔ گزارش سازمان ملل در سال ۲۰۲۴، ...». نوشتن «باور بر این است که» بدون منبع، در نوشتار دانشگاهی ضعف است.
۵. **واژه‌های فنی را با معادل دقیق بیاورید:** *to decommission* ← «از رده خارج کردن»، *to refurbish* ← «بازسازی کردن»، *to offset* ← «جبران کردن انتشار».
۶. **واحدها را به فارسی بنویسید و یکا را نگه دارید:** «بیست میلی‌ثانیه»، «۱٫۵ واحد PUE»، «کیلوگرم کربن دی‌اکسید».
۷. **سرواژه‌ها را یکدست نگه دارید:** PUE، ESG، GDPR و IRB را ترجمه نکنید؛ در نخستین کاربرد معادل فارسی را در پرانتز بیاورید.
۸. **از این ترجمه‌های تحت‌اللفظی بپرهیزید:** *dark data* ← «دادهٔ بی‌استفاده» (نه «دادهٔ تاریک» به‌معنای پنهان)، *right to repair* ← «حق تعمیر» (نه «حق ترمیم ماشین»)، *data subject* ← «صاحب داده/فرد موضوع داده» (نه «موضوع داده»).

### 7. Grammar Focus / نکتهٔ گرامری

**موضوع: ابزارهای انسجام و لحن محتاطانه (connectors and hedging)**

**توضیح فارسی:**

دو ابزار، نوشتار دانشگاهی را از نوشتار تبلیغاتی جدا می‌کند: **ربط‌دهنده** رابطهٔ منطقی میان دو جمله را نشان می‌دهد و **لحن محتاطانه** ادعا را به اندازهٔ شواهد محدود می‌کند.

**ربط‌دهنده‌ها بر پایهٔ نقش:** افزودن (*moreover, furthermore, in addition*)؛ تضاد (*however, nevertheless, whereas, in contrast, on the other hand*)؛ نتیجه (*therefore, consequently, as a result*)؛ تسلیم (*although, even though, while*)؛ مثال (*for instance, such as*)؛ ترتیب (*first, then, finally*)؛ جمع‌بندی (*in short, to conclude*). نکتهٔ نقطه‌گذاری: *however*, *therefore* و *consequently* پیش از خود ویرگول می‌گیرند، ولی *although* ویرگول نمی‌گیرد.

**لحن محتاطانه** با چند سازوکار ساخته می‌شود: ① فعل وجهی (*may, might, could*)؛ ② فعل گزارشی (*appear, seem, suggest, tend to, indicate*)؛ ③ قید (*arguably, broadly, roughly, approximately, generally*)؛ ④ عبارت (*it is likely that, in most cases, to some extent, on the available evidence*)؛ ⑤ مقدار تقریبی (*some, many, a minority of*) به‌جای *all* و *every*.

دو خطای رایج فارسی‌زبانان: ① آوردن *but* بعد از *although* ✗ *Although the tool was accurate, but it was unequal* ← درست: *Although the tool was accurate, it was unequal*؛ ② ادعای مطلق با فعل قطعی ✗ *This proves that all users ignore notices* ← درست: *This suggests that many users tend to ignore notices.*

در یک گزارش فنی، دو ابزار را با هم به کار ببرید. یک ربط‌دهندهٔ تضاد همراه با یک جملهٔ محتاطانه، مانند *However, the evidence suggests that the effect is small in one subgroup.* و یک منبع همراه با یک عدد، مانند *According to the vendor's own report, the tool was validated on two datasets.* این ترکیب به خواننده نشان می‌دهد که نویسنده هم تفاوت را دیده است و هم مرز ادعای خود را می‌شناسد.

**جدول ۱ — ربط‌دهنده‌ها بر پایهٔ نقش:**

| نقش | ربط‌دهنده | نمونه |
|---|---|---|
| افزودن | moreover / furthermore / in addition | Moreover, the vendor had asked for ten years of history. |
| تضاد | however / nevertheless / whereas | However, the same score meant a different risk. |
| نتیجه | therefore / consequently / as a result | Consequently, the notice was rewritten. |
| تسلیم | although / even though / while | Although the tool was accurate, it was unequal in effect. |
| مثال | for instance / such as | Two variables, such as cost and postcode, acted as proxies. |
| جمع‌بندی | in short / to conclude | In short, the number must have a boundary. |

**جدول ۲ — سازوکارهای لحن محتاطانه:**

| سازوکار | نمونه | اثر |
|---|---|---|
| فعل وجهی | The rule may fail for a small group. | احتمال را باز می‌گذارد |
| فعل گزارشی | The data suggest that the score differs by group. | ادعا را به شواهد می‌بندد |

| منبع | According to a 2024 report, ... | مسئولیت را به منبع می‌سپارد |
| مقدار تقریبی | A minority of users read the notice. | تعمیم مطلق را رد می‌کند |

2. با این حال، وقتی متغیر نیابتی نابرابر باشد، امتیاز یکسان ممکن است ریسک متفاوتی معنا دهد.
3. بر پایهٔ گزارش یک سازمان بین‌المللی در سال ۲۰۲۴، زبالهٔ الکترونیکی سریع‌تر از هر جریان دیگری رشد می‌کند.
4. در نتیجه، مرکز این ابزار را با سه شرط پذیرفت.
5. به‌طور کلی، محتمل است که بزرگ‌ترین صرفه‌جویی از خاموش کردن دستگاه‌ها در شب آمده باشد.
6. علاوه بر این، یک اقلیت از کاربران می‌گویند که اطلاعیهٔ حریم خصوصی را می‌خوانند.
7. در حالی که صورت‌حساب یک‌پنجم کاهش یافت، انتشار گزارش‌شده کمی بیشتر کاهش یافت.
8. واضح نیست که آیا این اثر در درمانگاه دیگری کوچک می‌بود.
9. تقریباً یک‌سوم انرژی صرف سرمایش و توزیع شده است.
10. در جمع‌بندی، ادعایی که مرزش نوشته نشده باشد، شاهد نیست.

### 9. Writing Task / تمرین نگارش

**موضوع: A Decision I Would Defend — تصمیمی که از آن دفاع می‌کنم**

یک تصمیم اخلاقی یا زیست‌محیطی را انتخاب کنید که **خودتان** در یک پروژه، کارگاه یا درس گرفته‌اید: انتشار یک ابزار، جمع‌آوری یک مجموعهٔ داده، انتخاب یک سرویس، یا حذف یک فایل. سپس دربارهٔ آن بنویسید:

- آن تصمیم چه بود و چه کسی آن را می‌خواست؟
- چه کسی از آن سود برد و چه کسی آسیب دید؟ (فهرست ذی‌نفعان را بنویسید)
- چه چیزی را اندازه گرفتید و چه چیزی را نتوانستید اندازه بگیرید؟
- چه ادعایی می‌کنید و **مرز** آن ادعا کجاست؟ (با لحن محتاطانه)
- یک ادعای مطلق بنویسید و سپس همان را به شکل محتاطانه بازنویسی کنید.
- اگر این تجربه تکرار شود، چه چیزی را عوض می‌کنید؟

**شرایط نگارش:**

- نوشته بین **۲۰۰ تا ۳۰۰ واژه** باشد.
- دست‌کم **۵ ربط‌دهنده** از نقش‌های مختلف داشته باشد (تضاد، نتیجه، تسلیم، افزودن، جمع‌بندی).
- دست‌کم **۴ سازوکار لحن محتاطانه** در متن باشد (فعل وجهی، فعل گزارشی، قید، عبارت، یا نام منبع).
- هیچ ادعای مطلقی بدون تضعیف در متن نماند.
- دست‌کم **۸ واژه** از جدول‌های واژگان بخش ۲ استفاده شود.

### 10. Mini Project / پروژهٔ کوچک

**انشا (نیمه‌تحقیقی): پروندهٔ اخلاقی و زیست‌محیطی یک سرویس واقعی**

یک سرویس یا ابزار واقعی را انتخاب کنید که **خودتان** هر روز از آن استفاده می‌کنید (یک اپلیکیشن موبایل، سامانهٔ آموزشی دانشگاه، یک سرویس ابری، یا یک ابزار هوش مصنوعی) و یک پروندهٔ کوتاه بنویسید:

- **داده:** این سرویس چه داده‌هایی جمع می‌کند و کدام‌یک از آن‌ها واقعاً برای کار لازم است؟ کدام را حذف می‌کنید؟
- **ذی‌نفعان:** چه کسی سود می‌برد، چه کسی ممکن است آسیب ببیند و کدام گروه آسیب‌پذیرتر است؟
- **سوگیری احتمالی:** یک متغیر نیابتی ممکن را نام ببرید و بگویید اگر جای ویژگی اصلی را بگیرد چه اتفاقی می‌افتد.
- **شکل دستی:** زنجیرهٔ هزینه را روی کاغذ بکشید: دستگاه ← شبکه ← سرور ← خنک‌کننده ← برق. روی هر پیکان یک عدد یا یک علامت پرسش بنویسید.
- **جدول دستی مقایسه:** جدولی با چهار سطر بکشید و دو شیوهٔ استفاده را مقایسه کنید (مثلاً استفادهٔ روزمره در برابر استفادهٔ بهینه): هزینه، انرژی، حریم خصوصی، دشواری.
- **دو ادعا و یک محدودیت:** دو ادعا که از داده‌های خودتان می‌توانید بسازید و یک ادعا که نمی‌توانید بنویسید، هر سه با لحن محتاطانه.
- **یک اقدام:** یک تغییر کوچک که از این هفته اجرا می‌کنید و یک عدد برای سنجش آن.

**شرایط نگارش:**

- گزارش را **دست‌نویس روی کاغذ** بنویسید و در **جلسهٔ بعد** به مدرس تحویل دهید.
- دست‌کم **۲۵۰ واژه** باشد.
- دست‌کم **۶ ربط‌دهنده** از نقش‌های مختلف و **۴ سازوکار لحن محتاطانه** داشته باشد.
- طرح دستی زنجیرهٔ هزینه و جدول دستی مقایسه در گزارش باشد.
- دست‌کم **۱۲ واژه** از جدول‌های واژگان بخش ۲ در گزارش به‌کار رفته باشد.

**واژه‌های یاری‌دهنده:** data minimisation · purpose limitation · legitimate interest · stakeholder · vulnerable group · proxy metric · unintended consequence · externality · carbon intensity · embodied carbon · idle power · repairability

### 11. Did You Know? / آیا می‌دانستید؟

1. **The idea of green computing was promoted in 1992 by a labelling programme in the United States, which marked computers that used less electricity.**
   ایدهٔ رایانش سبز در سال ۱۹۹۲ با برنامهٔ برچسب‌گذاری در ایالات متحده ترویج شد؛ برنامه‌ای که کامپیوترهای کم‌مصرف‌تر را نشانه‌گذاری می‌کرد.

2. **The world generated about 62 million tonnes of electronic waste in 2022, and only about a fifth of it was collected formally, according to a United Nations report published in 2024.**
   جهان در سال ۲۰۲۲ حدود ۶۲ میلیون تُن زبالهٔ الکترونیکی تولید کرد و به گزارش سازمان ملل در ۲۰۲۴ تنها حدود یک‌پنجم آن به‌صورت رسمی جمع‌آوری شد.

3. **Research published in 2020 estimated that streaming one hour of video produces roughly tens of grams of carbon dioxide — far more than a small download and far less than a short flight.**
   پژوهشی که در ۲۰۲۰ منتشر شد برآورد کرد پخش یک ساعت ویدیو حدود چند ده گرم کربن دی‌اکسید تولید می‌کند؛ بسیار بیش از یک دانلود کوچک و بسیار کمتر از یک پرواز کوتاه.

4. **A widely discussed study in 2019 found that a health algorithm used in hospitals underestimated the needs of one patient group, because it used the cost of care as a proxy for illness.**
   پژوهشی که در ۲۰۱۹ بسیار بحث‌برانگیز شد نشان داد الگوریتمی درمانی در بیمارستان‌ها نیاز یک گروه از بیماران را کم‌برآورد می‌کرد، چون هزینهٔ درمان را به‌جای شدت بیماری به کار می‌برد.

5. **In 2021, the European Union began to require spare parts for certain appliances, and similar right-to-repair rules were discussed in several countries afterwards.**
   در سال ۲۰۲۱ اتحادیهٔ اروپا ارائهٔ قطعات یدکی برای برخی لوازم خانگی را الزامی کرد و پس از آن قواعد مشابه «حق تعمیر» در چند کشور بررسی شد.

6. **Industry surveys in 2023 reported an average power usage effectiveness of about 1.5 in data centres, which means that roughly a third of the energy was spent on cooling and distribution.**
   بررسی‌های صنعتی در ۲۰۲۳ میانگین بهره‌وری مصرف توان را در مراکز داده حدود ۱٫۵ گزارش کردند؛ یعنی تقریباً یک‌سوم انرژی صرف سرمایش و توزیع شده است.

7. **In 2022, several cloud providers announced services that move flexible computing to the hours when the local electricity grid is cleaner.**
   در سال ۲۰۲۲ چند ارائه‌دهندهٔ ابری خدماتی معرفی کردند که رایانش انعطاف‌پذیر را به ساعت‌هایی منتقل می‌کند که شبکهٔ برق محلی پاک‌تر است.

8. **Regulators in several regions announced rules against vague environmental claims in 2023, because a large share of advertised green promises could not be checked.**
   نهادهای تنظیم‌گر در چند منطقه در سال ۲۰۲۳ قواعدی علیه ادعاهای مبهم زیست‌محیطی اعلام کردند، چون بخش بزرگی از وعده‌های سبز قابل بررسی نبود.
