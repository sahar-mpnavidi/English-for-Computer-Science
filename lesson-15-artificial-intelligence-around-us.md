### 3. Reading / متن اصلی

**عنوان متن: The Helpers That Learn (یاری‌گرانی که یاد می‌گیرند)**

**[1]** Artificial intelligence is not a machine from a science-fiction film. It is a set of tools that already help us every day: a phone that unlocks itself when it sees your face, a map that chooses the fastest road, a program that suggests a film, and an assistant that writes the first version of a text.

**[2]** The most important idea behind these tools is machine learning. In the old style, a programmer wrote the rules by hand: if the number is bigger than ten, do this. In machine learning, nobody writes the rule; the program learns the rule from examples, and this is why the quality of the training data decides almost everything. This change moved the work of a programmer from writing rules to preparing examples, and it also moved the hardest job into the data itself.

**[3]** A model is trained on examples, which are collected in a dataset. Each example has a vector of numbers, and a matrix keeps them together. If the examples have the right answers, the work is called supervised learning. If they have no answers at all, it is unsupervised learning, and the program must find the pattern alone.

**[4]** Another style is reinforcement learning, in which an agent tries an action, sees the result, and learns from the reward. This is the method that made computers strong at games, and it is also used for robots that must move in the real world.

**[5]** A neural network is built from many small units that are called neurons. The neurons are put in layers, and each layer changes the numbers a little. Deep learning simply means a network with many layers, and it works best when there is a lot of data and a lot of computing power.

**[6]** Nobody can read a deep network like a book. The model computes a probability for every possible answer, and the highest probability becomes the prediction. This is why an honest team also reports accuracy, precision, and recall: accuracy tells you how often the model is right, precision asks how many of its positive answers were correct, and recall asks how many of the real cases it found. A high accuracy number alone can hide a serious problem: a model that says "no" to everything may still look good on a dataset where most answers are "no".

**[7]** A model that learns the examples too well will fail on new ones; this problem is called overfitting. The real goal is to generalize: a classifier that was trained on one hospital should also work in another one, which is a much harder test than the first.

**[8]** If the answer is a name, the job is classification, and if the answer is a number, the job is regression, which predicts values such as price or temperature. Both jobs need a clean dataset, and both suffer when the data is biased, because a bad pattern in the data becomes a firm rule in the model.

**[9]** Modern text tools are generative. A chatbot is a program that talks with people in writing, and a large model of this kind is trained on a corpus of billions of words. Every word is turned into an embedding, which is a vector that keeps the meaning, so similar words sit near each other in the number space.

**[10]** When you write a prompt, the model does inference and predicts the next word again and again. A clear prompt gives a much better answer than a short one, and this small skill is now a real part of digital work.

**[11]** Generative tools can also synthesize a voice, transcribe a lecture, or turn a text into a picture. Speech tools decode a sound wave and encode it as words, and translation tools move the text from one language to another in a second.

**[12]** Vision is another large area. Computer vision is used for face recognition at a door, where a camera can recognize a face in less than a second, for reading number plates, and for finding a broken part on a factory line. A drone that flies over a field can count the plants, and a self-driving car uses cameras and maps that are updated all the time.

**[13]** Robotics joins the software with a body, and this is where the physical world makes the job harder. A robot that lifts boxes in a store must adapt when a box is in a strange place, and an autonomous machine must know its own limitation before it moves.

**[14]** Shopping and media use a different family of models. A recommender ranks thousands of films, songs, or products and recommends the best ones for one user. Every recommendation is personal. This system personalizes the page, and the same idea assists a doctor who is looking for the right article.

**[15]** Data work is not glamorous, but it decides the result. Human workers annotate the examples, tag each sentence with a topic, and curate the best of them. Engineers preprocess the text, remove dirty rows, and augment the dataset with small changes, so that the model sees more variety.

**[16]** Some teams mine old logs for hidden patterns, and some use experts who bring real domain expertise into the design. The first dataset is often small, and a small dataset is the fastest way to a biased model.

**[17]** After the first training, the team evaluates the model on new data. They fine-tune the parameters, align the answers with the rules of the company, and check whether the new version outperforms the old one. A model that cannot be evaluated cannot be trusted. The team also keeps an older version, so that a new model can always be compared with the one that it replaced.

**[18]** A good assistant can infer the meaning of a short question, distinguish two similar words, and estimate the right answer, but it cannot reason like a person, and it does not know the context of your life. If the model has no knowledge about a topic, it may hallucinate a confident answer instead of saying that it does not know.

**[19]** Trust is the real product. A trustworthy system shows its sources and its limitations, and a biased model can spread unfair decisions at a large scale. A deepfake that mimics a real voice is one of the newest examples, and misinformation travels faster than the correction that follows it.

**[20]** Copyright and fairness are part of the same discussion. A model that was trained on texts which belong to other people must respect their rights, and a system that makes decisions about people must be checked for fairness, because a biased rule is easy to copy and hard to notice.

**[21]** What can such a system really do? Every new capability arrives with a new question about responsibility. It can interact with a user in natural language, assist with a first draft, and supervise a boring, repetitive job. It cannot replace human judgment in a serious decision, and it cannot hold responsibility, because responsibility needs a person who can explain a choice.

**[22]** Mina uses an assistant for her English study. She writes a prompt, and the tool corrects her grammar, which saves her twenty minutes every day. Last week it hallucinated a word that does not exist, and she noticed it only because her teacher checked the text.

**[23]** Her friend uses a tutor that adapts to his level. The tutor, which was trained on a corpus of school texts, gives him easier tasks when he fails twice. This kind of system personalizes learning, and it also teaches the student to check every answer that looks strange.

**[24]** In medicine, a model that reads scans can help a doctor who has too many images to study. The doctor still makes the final judgment, because the tool has no expertise about the patient's family or history, and a wrong prediction costs much more than a slow one.

**[25]** In a factory, a vision system finds a broken part that a tired worker may miss. The robot that lifts the part is supervised by a human, who stops the line when something looks unusual. Productivity grows, and the workforce learns new skills instead of losing the old ones.

**[26]** This is why some jobs change instead of disappearing. Automation can raise unemployment in one place and create new work in another, and creativity is still hard to copy. The honest answer is that the work will change, and the people who keep learning will change with it.

**[27]** What are the common mistakes? A team collects a biased dataset, forgets the context, and trusts the accuracy number without asking about fairness. Another team deploys a model that nobody can evaluate, and a third team thinks that a bigger model is always better. In most projects, the data is the real limitation, not the algorithm.

**[28]** So, how should we think about artificial intelligence? It is a set of intelligent tools that learn from examples, which can help us, surprise us, and also mislead us. A system which is powerful and a system which is trustworthy are not always the same thing, and the second one is the one that we really need.

**[29]** Some systems learn from data that people did not agree to share. Consent, privacy, and copyright are not only legal words; they decide whether a project can be published at all. A team that collects data without asking will lose the trust of its users, and trust, once lost, is slow to return.

**[30]** What will come next? Models will become cheaper, smaller, and more specialised, and more of them will run on the device in your pocket. An agent that reads your calendar and books a room is already possible, and an autonomous system that drives a car in a city is still far away. The next big question is not what machines can do, but what people will allow.

### 7. Grammar Focus / نکتهٔ گرامری

**موضوع: جمله‌های موصولی (relative clauses)**

**توضیح فارسی:**

با جملهٔ موصولی دو جمله را به هم وصل می‌کنیم و دربارهٔ یک اسم بیشتر توضیح می‌دهیم. ضمیر موصولی جای اسم تکرارشده را می‌گیرد: **who** برای اشخاص (*the engineer who built the model*)، **which** برای اشیا (*the program which reads the scans*)، **that** برای هر دو در بند تعریف‌کننده (*the tool that we use*), **whose** برای مالکیت (*the student whose data was used*) و **where** برای مکان (*the centre where the data is stored*).

دو نوع بند موصولی داریم. **بند تعریف‌کننده** اطلاعات **ضروری** را می‌دهد و **بدون کاما** نوشته می‌شود؛ اگر حذف شود، معنی جمله ناقص می‌شود: *The model that answers questions is new.* **بند توضیحی** اطلاعات **اضافی** می‌دهد و **بین دو کاما** می‌آید؛ در این نوع نمی‌توان از *that* استفاده کرد: *This model, which answers questions, is new.*

**حذف ضمیر موصولی:** اگر ضمیر در بند **مفعول** باشد، در بند تعریف‌کننده می‌توان آن را حذف کرد: *The model (that) we trained is fast.* اما اگر ضمیر **فاعل** بند باشد، حذف نمی‌شود: *The model that works fast is small.* (نه ~~The model works fast is small~~).

دو خطای رایج فارسی‌زبانان: ① تکرار کردن فاعل بعد از ضمیر موصولی («The book that I read it was good» ← ~~that I read it~~ / *that I read*)؛ ② استفاده از *that* در بند توضیحی با کاما («My teacher, that helped me, was kind» ← ~~that~~ / *who*).

| حالت | نمونه |
|---|---|
| ضمیر مفعولی: حذف ممکن است | The model (that) we trained is fast. |
| ضمیر فاعلی: حذف نمی‌شود | The model that works fast is small. |

### 9. Writing Task / تمرین نگارش

**موضوع: A Tool That Helps Me — ابزاری که به من کمک می‌کند**

دربارهٔ یک ابزار هوشمند (دستیار، ربات گفتگو، مترجم، مرورگر با پیشنهاد، سامانهٔ توصیه) که خودتان استفاده می‌کنید بنویسید. این موارد را بگنجانید:

- آن ابزار چه کاری انجام می‌دهد و شما از کجا آن را می‌شناسید؟
- آن را برای چه کاری به کار می‌برید و چقدر وقت شما را ذخیره می‌کند؟
- یک بار که اشتباه کرد یا پاسخ نادرست داد و شما چگونه فهمیدید.
- نظر شما دربارهٔ محدودیت‌های آن ابزار.

**شرایط نگارش:**

- نوشته بین **۱۲۰ تا ۲۰۰ واژه** باشد.
- دست‌کم **۴ بند موصولی تعریف‌کننده** و **۲ بند موصولی توضیحی** داشته باشد.
- دست‌کم یک بار *whose* و یک بار *where* به کار ببرید.
- دست‌کم **۱۰ واژه** از جدول‌های واژگان بخش ۲ استفاده شود.

### 10. Mini Project / پروژهٔ کوچک

**انشا (نیمه‌تحقیقی): گزارش یک کاربرد هوش مصنوعی در زندگی من**

یک کاربرد واقعی هوش مصنوعی را که **خودتان** با آن سر و کار دارید انتخاب کنید (سامانهٔ توصیه، دستیار متنی، ترجمهٔ ماشینی، بازشناسی چهره، دستیار صوتی، فیلتر ایمیل) و گزارش کوتاهی بنویسید:

- **کارکرد:** آن سامانه چه کاری انجام می‌دهد؟ سه کاربرد واقعی خود را بنویسید.
- **نوع یادگیری:** به نظر شما آن سامانه بانظارت، بدون‌نظارت یا تقویتی است؟ چرا؟ (حدس شما کافی است، دلیل مهم است.)
- **نتیجه:** یک نمونهٔ درست و یک نمونهٔ نادرست از کار آن سامانه بنویسید (مثلاً ترجمهٔ غلط یا پیشنهاد نامناسب).
- **اعتماد:** چه چیزی آن سامانه را قابل اعتماد یا غیرقابل اعتماد می‌کند؟ (منبع، شفافیت، سوگیری، توهم)
- **جمله‌های موصولی:** شش جمله بنویسید که در آن‌ها از *that*، *which*، *who*، *whose* و *where* استفاده شده باشد.
- **پیشنهاد:** دو راه برای بهتر شدن آن سامانه پیشنهاد بدهید.

**شرایط نگارش:**

- گزارش را **دست‌نویس روی کاغذ** بنویسید و در **جلسهٔ بعد** به مدرس تحویل دهید.
- دست‌کم **۲۰۰ واژه** باشد.
- دست‌کم **۶ بند موصولی** (چهار تعریف‌کننده و دو توضیحی) داشته باشد.
- دست‌کم **۱۲ واژه** از جدول‌های واژگان بخش ۲ در گزارش باشد.

**واژه‌های یاری‌دهنده:** machine learning · training data · classifier · prediction · accuracy · bias · prompt · chatbot · generative · trustworthy · limitation

### 11. Did You Know? / آیا می‌دانستید؟

   اصطلاح «هوش مصنوعی» نخستین بار در سال ۱۹۵۶ در کارگاهی کوچک و تابستانی در ایالات متحده به کار رفت.

2. **Machine translation was already one of the first big projects, and a famous test of it was made in 1966.**
   ترجمهٔ ماشینی از نخستین پروژه‌های بزرگ بود و آزمونی مشهور از آن در سال ۱۹۶۶ انجام شد.

3. **A computer that played chess beat the world champion in 1997, long before the same method was used for language.**
   کامپیوتری که شطرنج بازی می‌کرد در سال ۱۹۹۷ قهرمان جهان را شکست داد، بسیار پیش از آنکه همان روش برای زبان به کار رود.

4. **In 2012, deep learning made a large jump in image recognition, and this is often called the start of the modern period.**
   در سال ۲۰۱۲ یادگیری عمیق جهش بزرگی در بازشناسی تصویر ایجاد کرد و این را اغلب آغاز دورهٔ امروزی می‌نامند.

   گزارش‌های صنعت در سال ۲۰۲۳ نشان داد بیشتر شرکت‌هایی که از هوش مصنوعی استفاده می‌کنند، آن را برای کارهای باریک و مشخص به کار می‌برند، نه برای هوش عمومی.

   پژوهشی در سال ۲۰۲۳ نشان داد مردم وقتی می‌دانند پاسخ را یک ماشین نوشته، کمتر به آن اعتماد می‌کنند، حتی وقتی پاسخ یکی است.
