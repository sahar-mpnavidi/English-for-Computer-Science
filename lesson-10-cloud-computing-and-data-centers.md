### 1. Lesson Objectives / اهداف درس

- رایانش ابری و تفاوت آن با مدل در محل سازمان (on-premises) را توضیح دهید.

- مفاهیم کشش‌پذیری، پرداخت به‌میزان مصرف، توزیع بار و انتقال خودکار به سرور پشتیبان را توضیح دهید.

### 3. Reading / متن اصلی

**عنوان متن: The Factory of Computing Power (کارخانهٔ توان محاسباتی)**

**[1]** Cloud computing means that you use computing power over the internet instead of buying your own machines. You do not need a server in your room; you rent a virtual machine from a vendor for one hour or one year. This idea has changed the architecture of almost every modern company.

**[2]** Before the cloud, a company built its own server room. This model is called on-premises: the hardware, the cooling, and the maintenance were the work of the company. Today many companies use a hybrid model: some systems stay on-premises, and other systems run in the cloud.

**[3]** Where does the cloud live? In data centers: large facilities with thousands of servers. A data center is not an office; it is a building with heavy power lines, strong cooling systems, and very few people inside. Big vendors have data centers in many countries.

**[4]** Inside a data center, the servers stand in tall racks, one above another. People walk in narrow aisles between the racks. A modern rack holds blade servers: thin machines that save space. Many servers work together in a cluster, so one machine can fail without stopping the service.

**[5]** A data center must control its air. Cooling keeps the machines from getting too hot, and a good airflow system moves cool air to the machines. The team also watches the humidity and the temperature of every room: too much humidity damages the parts.

**[6]** Power is the first problem of a data center. The grid gives electricity, but the center also has a generator for emergencies. Batteries hold enough power for a few minutes, so the generator has time to start. Spare parts and a fast replacement plan keep the machines alive.

**[7]** Nobody can watch a thousand servers by hand. Monitoring tools send every metric to one dashboard, and every important event goes into a log. When one number goes above a normal level, the system sends an alert to the team. Analytics help the engineers to see patterns before a fault happens.

**[8]** Security begins at the perimeter. Only people with a correct badge can enter, and some doors also check a biometric sign such as a fingerprint. Inside, the isolation of each customer is very important: one tenant must never see the data of another tenant. This separation is part of the cloud architecture.

**[9]** Companies must also follow rules. Regulation and compliance decide where data can live, and many customers ask for a certification before they sign a contract. Big providers offer different tiers of service, and every tier comes with an SLA: a written promise about availability.

**[10]** How does one machine become a hundred? Virtualization. A program on a strong server creates many virtual machines, and each one has its own system. These instances can start in a minute. Multi-tenant data centers use virtualization to serve thousands of customers with the same hardware.

**[11]** Cloud services come in three main forms. IaaS gives you the infrastructure: servers, storage, and the network. PaaS gives you a platform for building programs, so you do not manage the machines yourself. SaaS gives you the finished program, like an online office package. Most users meet the cloud as SaaS.

**[12]** The biggest promise of the cloud is elasticity. If a website has many visitors tonight, the company can add ten instances on-demand and pay only for the hours that it uses them. This model is called pay-as-you-go, and the billing follows the utilization. Good efficiency means that you pay for what you need.

**[13]** Storage works in a similar way. A company can keep its files in a bucket, and the provider makes copies in many places. This replication gives high durability, so a lost disk is not a disaster. A snapshot of the data is taken every night, and a template keeps the same configuration for every new server.

**[14]** How does a service stay online? A load balancer sends each request to a server with free capacity, so no single machine carries all the load. If one server stops, a failover moves its workload to another machine in seconds. A secondary site in a second region protects the company from a big disaster.

**[15]** Regions and zones are the geography of the cloud. A region is a large area, such as Europe, and each region has two or more zones: separate buildings with their own power and cooling. If a company needs short latency for its users, it will choose a nearby region; if regulation demands it, the data will stay inside one country.

**[16]** Modern teams automate as much as possible. Orchestration starts and stops instances without a human hand, and automation keeps every configuration the same. A serverless service is the next step: you write a small program, and the platform decides how many machines it needs. You pay only for the seconds when your program runs.

**[17]** Migration is the hardest part of cloud work. A company must move old programs, connect them to new services, and test the integration between the two worlds. A deployment plan lists every step, and provisioning gives each new instance the resources that it needs. A wrong migration can stop a business for days.

**[18]** Performance is not only a number; it is a promise. Engineers run a benchmark before they choose a vendor, and they measure the performance of every new release. A buffer holds data for a short time, and a partition divides a very large table into smaller parts. Every user session gets its own token.

**[19]** Money is the last piece. A policy about cost helps a company to keep its budget under control, and every license and subscription appears in the monthly bill. A small startup can begin with a free tier and pay more only when it grows. An audit of the bill often finds waste.

**[20]** What will the cloud look like in ten years? Most engineers believe that it will grow, and that more work will move to the edge: small data centers near the users. A company will keep a hybrid design, because some data must stay on-premises. Nobody knows the exact answer, but the direction is clear.

**[21]** Energy is the hidden cost of the cloud. A large data center consumes as much electricity as a small town, and its carbon footprint worries many engineers. Companies now buy renewable energy and put solar panels on the roof. Every small improvement in efficiency also reduces the consumption of power.

**[22]** Safety is not only about people. A data center has fire systems, and every room has smoke sensors on the ceiling. When the temperature rises too fast, the system acts before a fire can spread. Regular checks and a clear plan make an expansion safe as well.

**[23]** How does a team manage all of this? From a single console, an engineer can see every instance in every region. The console shows the metrics, the logs, and the monthly bill, and it can start a new virtual machine in one minute. This is the daily work of a cloud engineer.

**[24]** Mina and her classmates are going to build a small cloud project this semester. They are going to rent one virtual machine, put a database on it, and connect a simple website. Next week they are meeting their teacher to plan the architecture. They will use a free tier, because the budget is small.

**[25]** What will happen when the project starts? If the website is slow, they will add a second instance. If a disk fails, the snapshot will save their work, and the load balancer will send the traffic to the second machine. When they finish, they are going to write a short report about cost and efficiency.

**[26]** Cloud skills will be important for every student of computer science. A certification in one platform will help you in the job market, and a small startup will pay less for the cloud than for its own server room. The work will change, but the ideas of virtualization, isolation, and elasticity will stay.

**[27]** The cloud is a simple idea with a difficult engine: somebody else's computers, in a building far away, work for you every second. Behind one click there are racks, cooling, power, monitoring, and a large team. That is why the invisible engine of the modern internet is a data center.

**[28]** The cloud also has risks. A vendor lock-in means that a company cannot easily move to another provider, because its programs use special services. A security incident in one tenant can frighten thousands of customers. An outage in one region can stop a business for hours.

**[29]** Remote work and the cloud grew together. A team in three countries can share one platform and one configuration. The analytics of the logs show how people use the service, and a metric about performance helps the team to improve it. Nothing of this needs an office building.

**[30]** In the next lesson you will read about programming languages, but for now remember three ideas. First, the cloud is rental computing, and you will pay for what you use. Second, a data center is a factory of computing power with racks, cooling, and power. Third, elasticity and automation are going to decide the winners of the next ten years.

### 7. Grammar Focus / نکتهٔ گرامری

**موضوع: آینده با will و going to · قرارهای آینده · بندهای زمانی و شرطی**

**توضیح فارسی:**

دو راه اصلی برای سخن گفتن از آینده داریم. **will** + فعل ساده برای **پیش‌بینی** (*I think the cloud will grow.*)، **تصمیم لحظه‌ای** (*The phone is ringing. I'll answer it.*) و **وعده و پیشنهاد** (*I will help you with the configuration.*) به کار می‌رود. **going to** (am / is / are + going to + فعل ساده) برای **قصد و برنامهٔ از پیش تعیین‌شده** (*We are going to rent a virtual machine.*) و برای **پیش‌بینی بر پایهٔ شواهد** (*Look at that traffic! The network is going to become slow.*) استفاده می‌شود.

برای **قرارهای قطعی** در آیندهٔ نزدیک، حال استمراری هم به کار می‌رود: *Next week we are meeting our teacher to plan the architecture.*

**منفی:** *will not (won't)* و *am / is / are not going to*: *The cost won't grow. / We are not going to migrate this month.* **سؤالی:** *Will the platform grow? / Are you going to rent a server?* پاسخ کوتاه: *Yes, it will. / No, we aren't.*

**بندهای زمانی و شرطی:** بعد از *when, if, before, after, as soon as, until* فعل به شکل **حال ساده**می‌آید، حتی وقتی معنی آینده دارد: *When the project starts, we will add a second instance. / If the disk fails, the snapshot will save our work.* در جملهٔ اصلی می‌توانیم *will* یا *going to* داشته باشیم، اما در بند زمانی هرگز *will* نمی‌آید.

دو خطای رایج فارسی‌زبانان: ① آوردن *will* در بند زمانی («When I will finish, I will call you» ← ~~When I will finish~~ / *When I finish*، ...)؛ ② افزودن *to* یا *-s* به فعل بعد از *will* («I will to help» ← ~~will to help~~ / *will help*؛ «She will helps» ← ~~will helps~~ / *will help*).

**جدول ۱ — سه راه سخن گفتن از آینده:**

| شکل | ساختار | کاربرد | نمونه |
|---|---|---|---|
| will | will + فعل ساده | پیش‌بینی، تصمیم لحظه‌ای، وعده | The cloud will grow. / I'll answer it. |
| going to | am/is/are + going to + فعل ساده | برنامه و قصد، پیش‌بینی با شواهد | We are going to rent a server. |
| حال استمراری | am/is/are + فعل + ing | قرار قطعی در آیندهٔ نزدیک | We are meeting our teacher next week. |

**جدول ۲ — منفی، سؤالی و پاسخ کوتاه:**

| حالت | نمونه |
|---|---|
| منفی با will | The cost will not (won't) grow if we use a free tier. |
| منفی با going to | We are not going to migrate our data this month. |
| سؤالی | Will the platform grow? / Are you going to rent a server? |
| پاسخ کوتاه | Yes, it will. / No, we aren't. |

**جدول ۳ — بندهای زمانی و شرطی:**

| قاعده | نمونه |
|---|---|
| when + حال ساده | When the project starts, we will add a second instance. |
| if + حال ساده | If the disk fails, the snapshot will save our work. |
| before / after + حال ساده | Before we deploy the program, we will run a benchmark. |
| اشتباه رایج | ~~When I will finish, I will call you.~~ / When I finish, I will call you. |

### 9. Writing Task / تمرین نگارش

**موضوع: My Plans for the Cloud — برنامه‌های من برای فضای ابری**

یک نوشتهٔ کوتاه بنویسید و در آن از برنامه‌ها و پیش‌بینی‌های خود دربارهٔ فضای ابری بگویید. این موارد را بگنجانید:

- قصد دارید در سه ماه آینده چه مهارت ابری یاد بگیرید و چگونه؟ (دوره، ویدیو، تمرین روی یک سرویس رایگان)
- چه پروژه یا کاری را با فضای ابری انجام خواهید داد؟ (وب‌سایت شخصی، ذخیره‌سازی، پروژهٔ درسی)
- سه پیش‌بینی دربارهٔ آیندهٔ فضای ابری (هزینه، انرژی، امنیت، مشاغل).
- یک ریسک که به نظر شما مهم است (وابستگی به فروشنده، قطعی، هزینهٔ پنهان، حریم خصوصی).

**شرایط نگارش:**

- نوشته بین **۱۲۰ تا ۲۰۰ واژه** باشد.
- دست‌کم **۵ جمله با going to** (برنامه و قصد) و **۳ جمله با will** (پیش‌بینی) بنویسید.
- دست‌کم **۲ بند زمانی یا شرطی** با *when / if* داشته باشد.
- دست‌کم **۱۰ واژه** از جدول‌های واژگان بخش ۲ استفاده شود.

### 10. Mini Project / پروژهٔ کوچک

**انشا (نیمه‌تحقیقی): یک سرویس ابری در زندگی من**

یک سرویس ابری را که **خودتان** هر روز یا هر هفته از آن استفاده می‌کنید انتخاب کنید (فضای ذخیره‌سازی ابری، دفتر آنلاین، پلتفرم کد آنلاین، سرویس فیلم، ایمیل ابری) و دربارهٔ آن گزارش کوتاهی بنویسید:

- **کاربرد:** آن سرویس چه کاری برای شما انجام می‌دهد؟ سه کاربرد واقعی خود را بنویسید.
- **مدل:** به نظر شما این سرویس از کدام مدل است (IaaS، PaaS یا SaaS)؟ چرا؟
- **جدول مقایسه:** یک **جدول دستی** با چهار سطر بکشید و آن سرویس ابری را با روش محلی (روی دستگاه خودتان) در چهار مورد مقایسه کنید: هزینه، دسترس‌پذیری، حریم خصوصی، نگهداری.
- **یک مشکل واقعی:** چه مشکلی با آن داشته‌اید (قطعی، همگام‌سازی، از دست رفتن داده، هزینه) و چه کار کردید؟
- **برنامه و پیش‌بینی:** در ماه آینده چه مهارتی دربارهٔ این سرویس یاد خواهید گرفت (*going to*) و سه پیش‌بینی دربارهٔ آیندهٔ آن بنویسید (*will*).

**شرایط نگارش:**

- گزارش را **دست‌نویس روی کاغذ** بنویسید و در **جلسهٔ بعد** به مدرس تحویل دهید.
- دست‌کم **۲۰۰ واژه** باشد (بدون احتساب جدول).
- دست‌کم **۵ جمله با going to**، **۴ جمله با will** و **۲ بند زمانی یا شرطی** داشته باشد.
- دست‌کم **۱۲ واژه** از جدول‌های واژگان بخش ۲ در گزارش باشد.

**واژه‌های یاری‌دهنده:** cloud computing · virtual machine · instance · data center · elasticity · on-demand · pay-as-you-go · availability · durability · vendor · billing · footprint

### 11. Did You Know? / آیا می‌دانستید؟

1. **The term "cloud computing" became popular around 2006, when the first big consumer cloud services opened.**
   اصطلاح «رایانش ابری» حدود سال ۲۰۰۶ رایج شد، زمانی که نخستین سرویس‌های بزرگ ابری برای کاربران عادی باز شدند.

2. **Modern data centers try to cool their halls with cold outside air: in cold climates, some centers open their walls for most of the year.**
   مراکز دادهٔ امروزی می‌کوشند سالن‌های خود را با هوای سرد بیرون خنک کنند: در اقلیم‌های سرد، بعضی مراکز بیشتر سال درهای خود را باز می‌گذارند.

3. **A hyperscale data center can hold hundreds of thousands of servers, and building one can cost more than a billion dollars.**
   یک مرکز دادهٔ فوق‌مقیاس می‌تواند صدها هزار سرور داشته باشد و ساخت آن می‌تواند بیش از یک میلیارد دلار هزینه بردارد.

4. **According to industry reports in 2023, more than half of all business data was already stored in the cloud.**
   بر پایهٔ گزارش‌های صنعت در سال ۲۰۲۳، بیش از نیمی از داده‌های کسب‌وکارها از قبل در فضای ابری ذخیره شده بود.

5. **One modern rack of servers can hold more computing power than the whole world had in 1990.**
   یک رک سرور امروزی می‌تواند توان محاسباتی بیشتری از کل جهان در سال ۱۹۹۰ داشته باشد.

6. **Industry reports in 2023 showed that three big vendors held more than half of the world's cloud infrastructure market.**
   گزارش‌های صنعت در سال ۲۰۲۳ نشان داد سه فروشندهٔ بزرگ بیش از نیمی از بازار زیرساخت ابری جهان را در دست داشتند.

7. **Virtualization is not new: mainframe computers used it in the 1960s and 1970s, long before it came to ordinary servers.**
   مجازی‌سازی تازه نیست: کامپیوترهای مِین‌فریم در دههٔ ۱۹۶۰ و ۱۹۷۰ از آن استفاده می‌کردند، بسیار پیش از آنکه به سرورهای معمولی برسد.

8. **A survey in 2023 estimated that about 60 percent of companies used more than one cloud provider, a model called multi-cloud.**
   بررسی‌ای در سال ۲۰۲۳ برآورد کرد حدود ۶۰ درصد شرکت‌ها از بیش از یک ارائه‌دهندهٔ ابری استفاده می‌کنند؛ الگویی که «چند‌ابری» نامیده می‌شود.
