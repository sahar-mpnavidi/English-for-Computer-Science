### 1. Lesson Objectives / اهداف درس

- اجزای یک دستگاه هوشمند (ریزکنترلگر، مدار، حس‌گر، عملگر، فرم‌ور) را به انگلیسی توضیح دهید.
- تفاوت پردازش در «لبه» و پردازش در فضای ابری را بیان کنید.
- با *-ing* و *to + فعل* درست جمله بسازید و بدانید کدام فعل‌ها کدام شکل را می‌گیرند.
- بعد از افعال وجهی و *make / let / help* از فعل بدون *to* استفاده کنید.
- تفاوت *stop doing* با *stop to do* و *remember doing* با *remember to do* را نشان دهید.

### 3. Reading / متن اصلی

**عنوان متن: Small Machines That Never Sleep (ماشین‌های کوچکی که هرگز نمی‌خوابند)**

**[1]** The Internet of Things means connecting everyday objects to the internet. A lamp, a door, a kitchen appliance, a water meter, and even a shoe can become a smart device, and each of them can send data about itself. People like controlling their homes from a phone, and companies like measuring everything that happens in the factory.

**[2]** What is inside such a device? A miniature board holds a microcontroller, which is a very small computer, and a few sensors that sense light, heat, or movement. The device also needs an actuator that can do something physical: opening a valve, moving a lock, or dimming a lamp. The board sends one signal to actuate the part. A proximity sensor knows that a person is standing near the door, and a broken circuit stops the whole device.

**[3]** A small program called firmware runs inside the device. Updating that firmware is important, because a bug in it cannot be repaired with a screwdriver. Engineers also need to calibrate each sensor, so that a reading of 22 degrees is really 22 degrees. Calibration is boring work, but a wrong number is worse than no number.

**[4]** How do these devices talk to each other? Many of them use MQTT, which is a light protocol for small messages, and some use Zigbee, which uses very little power. Bluetooth is common for pairing a device with a phone, and Wi-Fi is used when a cable is impossible. Choosing a band is a real design decision.

**[5]** Setting up a new device takes three steps. First, the owner pairs it with a phone or a hub. Second, the device is registered or enrolled on a platform, and the platform provisions the keys that the device will use. Third, the owner calibrates it and mounts it in the right place.

**[6]** After the setup, the device starts sending readings. A sensor may send a number every minute, or every hour, and this interval is chosen by the designer. Sending a reading every second drains the battery, so low-power design is more important than speed in most IoT projects. A battery that is drained every week is not a design choice; it is a mistake that shows up in the first month.

**[7]** The device must decide what matters. If a reading goes above a threshold, the system sends an alert; below the threshold, it simply stores the number. A good threshold prevents flooding the network with useless data, and a bad one hides a real failure.

**[8]** Where should the data be processed? Sending everything to the cloud is simple but slow, so more teams are moving work to the edge: the device itself, or a small computer near it, decides and reacts at once. Fog computing takes the middle position, serving one building or one city.

**[9]** Keeping many devices alive is harder than installing one. A fleet of sensors needs provisioning, monitoring, and updating, and every failure must be seen at once. Every device must also be decommissioned at the end of its life. Teams that manage thousands of devices use a platform to diagnose a problem before they send a technician.

**[10]** Predictive maintenance is the reward for collecting all that data. Instead of replacing every part at the same time, the platform learns the normal behaviour of a machine, and it warns the team before a failure happens. This is why managers want granular, actionable data and not a big, noisy report. The platform compares today's behaviour with last month's data, and a small change is often the first sign of a coming failure.

**[11]** Old equipment is the quiet problem. A legacy meter may work for twenty years, but it cannot run new firmware, and it may not be interoperable with modern systems. Most companies retire these meters only when the parts are impossible to find, and when a protocol becomes obsolete, the whole generation must be replaced. Many factories must therefore integrate three generations of devices, and each generation speaks a different protocol.

**[12]** Interoperability is the hardest goal in this field. Fragmentation means that every vendor brings its own ecosystem, and a household can end up with five apps for five lamps. Customers who want one ecosystem usually accept higher prices, and the companies know this very well. Adoption is slower in old buildings, where every wall must be drilled.

**[13]** Security is the part that people forget. An unattended device in a public place is vulnerable, and an exposed port is an open door for an attacker. Every device must authorize its users, and every engineer should avoid using the same default password on a thousand machines. Authorizing each new device is boring work, but it is the cheapest security control in the whole project.

**[14]** The physical layer also matters. Devices in a factory are often rugged and waterproof, while a homeowner wants compact, silent devices that are never visible. A strong device is not always a small one, and a small device is not always a dependable one.

**[15]** Power decides the design in most projects. A device that conserves energy will use a small battery for years, so engineers balance the sending interval, the band, and the sensitivity of the sensor. Sustainability is not a slogan here; it is a battery that lasts.

**[16]** For the user, IoT is about convenience and comfort. The thermostat adjusts the temperature before they arrive, the lamp brightens when they enter the room, and a geofence turns everything off when they leave. One tap can toggle the whole scene, and every family can customize its own scenes. A small beacon in a shop sends a short signal, so the app knows where the customer is standing. These systems react to a person, not to a schedule.

**[17]** A wearable is a special case, because a person carries it and never leaves it on a desk. A smartwatch counts steps, measures the heart rate, and tells its owner to move. Wearing a device for a week teaches you more about your own habits than any report.

**[18]** Mina is building a small IoT project for her class, and she is learning a lot by doing it. She started by choosing the board, and she had to assemble it herself. She enjoys writing the firmware herself. She decided to mount three sensors in the lab, and she hopes to show the project at the end of the term.

**[19]** Her first problem was pairing. She forgot to authorize the new sensor on the platform, so the device could not send anything. Then she tried rebooting the device, and that did not help. Finally she remembered to enroll it again, and the readings arrived at once.

**[20]** Her second problem was power. The sensor was draining its battery in two days, because she was sending a reading every second. She changed the interval to five minutes and started conserving energy, and the same battery then lasted for weeks.

**[21]** Her third problem was noise. Every reading was sent as an alert, so the dashboard was full of useless numbers. She avoided flooding the network by adding a threshold, and the app became actionable again. Now a real problem is easy to see.

**[22]** What do beginners usually get wrong? They try to connect everything at once, and they forget to calibrate the sensors. They keep using the default password, and they avoid updating the firmware, because the first update always looks dangerous. Most of these mistakes are cheap to fix at the start.

**[23]** Before you install anything, plan three things: the place, the power, and the data. Mount the device where the reading is correct, choose a battery that fits the lifetime of the project, and decide the interval before the first test. A careful plan saves weeks of troubleshooting, and it also decides how you will expand the network later.

**[24]** Testing is the part that nobody enjoys, but it decides the result. You must diagnose the first failure carefully, and you should troubleshoot one device instead of ten. A networked device is only as safe as the network that it uses. When a device stops behaving, swap it with a working one and compare the readings; the difference usually shows the problem. Sometimes the fastest diagnosis is simply swapping the sensor with a new one and watching the readings change.

**[25]** What about privacy? A device that stays in your home collects a picture of your life, and an always-on microphone collects much more. Every owner must decide what may be collected, and every company must protect what it stores. Visibility inside the company is not the same as visibility inside your bedroom.

**[26]** Where is this field going? Engineers expect more pervasive and more invisible systems: devices will become part of the walls and of our clothes, and they will work without asking for attention. The word for this is maturity, and maturity is boring, which is exactly what a good system should be.

**[27]** The risk is that hype hides the work. It is easy to advertise a smart lamp and hard to keep ten thousand devices dependable for ten years. The teams that succeed are not the ones with the biggest dreams; they are the ones that plan for failure, power, and security from the first day. The complexity of a large deployment is the real enemy.

**[28]** So, what should you remember? The Internet of Things is about sensing, sending, and acting, with a small computer inside a small object. If you keep the firmware updated, choose the interval carefully, and watch the security of every connected thing, your project will survive its first winter. If you ignore these three rules, even a beautiful device will stop behaving.

**[29]** Scale changes everything. A company that manages ten devices uses a spreadsheet; a company that manages ten thousand devices needs a platform, a registration list, and a plan for every firmware version. At that scale, one small mistake is repeated ten thousand times, so testing on one device saves a week of work later.

**[30]** And what about the people who live with these devices? An older person may forget to charge a watch, and a child may wear a tracker to school. A good design does not ask for attention: it behaves quietly, it warns only when it must, and it tells the truth about its own battery. That is the difference between a gadget and a dependable tool.

### 7. Grammar Focus / نکتهٔ گرامری

**موضوع: مصدر با to و اسم مصدر با -ing (gerunds and infinitives)**

**توضیح فارسی:**

در انگلیسی بعد از فعل‌ها دو شکل رایج داریم: **اسم مصدر با -ing** (gerund) و **مصدر با to** (to-infinitive).

**اسم مصدر (-ing)** بعد از این گروه‌ها می‌آید: ① **حروف اضافه**: *good at measuring*, *instead of sending*, *before installing*؛ ② فعل‌هایی مانند *enjoy, avoid, finish, keep, suggest, consider, mind, practice*: *She enjoys writing the firmware.* / *He avoids flooding the network.*؛ ③ به‌عنوان فاعل جمله: *Sending data every second drains the battery.*

**مصدر با to** بعد از این گروه‌ها می‌آید: ① فعل‌هایی مانند *want, need, decide, plan, hope, try, learn, agree, promise, expect*: *She decided to mount three sensors.* / *I hope to show the project.*؛ ② صفت‌ها: *easy to use*, *important to check*؛ ③ برای **بیان هدف**: *We use a sensor to measure the temperature.*

**فعل بدون to** بعد از افعال وجهی (*can, must, should*) و بعد از *make / let / help* با مفعول می‌آید: *It makes the system stop.* / *Let me check the readings.* / *It helps the device save power.*

**سه تفاوت مهم** را با دقت یاد بگیرید: ① *stop doing* = کار را رها کردن (*He stopped sending data.*) در برابر *stop to do* = توقف کردن تا کاری انجام دهد (*He stopped to check the device.*)؛ ② *remember doing* = به یاد آوردن کاری که انجام شده (*I remember installing it.*) در برابر *remember to do* = یادت باشد انجامش دهی (*Remember to update the firmware.*)؛ ③ *try doing* = امتحان کردن یک روش، در برابر *try to do* = تلاش کردن.

دو خطای رایج فارسی‌زبانان: ① آوردن *to* بعد از فعل‌هایی که *-ing* می‌گیرند («I enjoy to read» ← ~~enjoy to read~~ / *enjoy reading*)؛ ② آوردن *-ing* بعد از فعل‌هایی که *to* می‌گیرند («I want going home» ← ~~want going~~ / *want to go*).

**جدول ۱ — اسم مصدر (-ing):**

| حالت | نمونه |

| حرف اضافه + -ing | good at adjusting · instead of sending · before installing |
| فعل + -ing | enjoy writing · avoid flooding · keep sending · finish pairing |
| فاعل جمله | Sending data costs very little. |

**جدول ۲ — مصدر با to:**

| حالت | نمونه |
|---|---|
| فعل + to | want to know · decide to install · plan to upgrade |
| صفت + to | easy to use · important to check |
| برای هدف | We use a sensor to measure the temperature. |

**جدول ۳ — بدون to و سه تفاوت مهم:**

| ساخت | نمونه | معنی |
|---|---|---|
| وجهی / make / let / help + فعل ساده | must check · make it stop · let me see | بدون to |
| stop doing | He stopped sending data. | ارسال را رها کرد |
| stop to do | He stopped to check the device. | ایستاد تا بررسی کند |
| remember doing / remember to do | I remember installing it. / Remember to update the firmware. | به یاد آوردن / یادت باشد |

### 10. Mini Project / پروژهٔ کوچک

**انشا (نیمه‌تحقیقی): گزارش یک دستگاه هوشمند یا یک سامانهٔ متصل**

یک دستگاه هوشمند یا سامانهٔ متصل را که **خودتان** دیده‌اید یا از آن استفاده می‌کنید انتخاب کنید (ساعت هوشمند، ترموستات هوشمند، کنتور هوشمند، چراغ هوشمند، پوشیدنی ورزشی، سامانهٔ متصل در محل کار) و گزارش کوتاهی بنویسید:

- **اجزا:** درون آن چه چیزی است؟ ریزکنترلگر، حس‌گر، عملگر، فرم‌ور و اتصال آن را توضیح دهید.
- **اتصال:** به نظر شما از چه فناوری‌ای استفاده می‌کند (MQTT، زیگبی، وای‌فای، بلوتوث) و چرا؟
- **داده:** چه داده‌ای جمع می‌کند، با چه فاصلهٔ زمانی، و چه آستانه‌ای برای هشدار منطقی است؟
- **نیرو:** باتری یا برق؟ عمر مفید آن چقدر است و برای مصرف کمتر چه می‌شود کرد؟
- **امنیت و حریم خصوصی:** چه کسی به داده دسترسی دارد و چه خطری جدی است؟
- **جمله‌های گرامری:** شش جمله بنویسید؛ دو مورد با **مصدر با to**، دو مورد با **اسم مصدر (-ing)** و دو مورد با فعل **بدون to**.
- **پیشنهاد:** دو پیشنهاد برای بهتر شدن آن دستگاه بنویسید.

**شرایط نگارش:**

- گزارش را **دست‌نویس روی کاغذ** بنویسید و در **جلسهٔ بعد** به مدرس تحویل دهید.
- دست‌کم **۲۰۰ واژه** باشد.
- دست‌کم **۶ جمله با to + فعل**، **۵ جمله با -ing** و **۳ جمله با فعل بدون to** داشته باشد.
- دست‌کم **۱۲ واژه** از جدول‌های واژگان بخش ۲ در گزارش باشد.

**واژه‌های یاری‌دهنده:** smart device · sensor · actuator · firmware · pairing · threshold · interval · battery · edge · vulnerable · interoperability · dependable

### 11. Did You Know? / آیا می‌دانستید؟

1. **The idea of connecting small computers to objects is old: a famous example from the 1980s was a machine that reported the number of bottles in a shop.**
   ایدهٔ وصل کردن کامپیوترهای کوچک به اشیا قدیمی است: نمونه‌ای مشهور در دههٔ ۱۹۸۰ ماشینی بود که شمار بطری‌های یک فروشگاه را گزارش می‌کرد.

2. **The short term "Internet of Things" became popular around 2009, although the technology itself is older than the name.**
   اصطلاح کوتاه «اینترنت اشیا» حدود سال ۲۰۰۹ رایج شد، هرچند خودِ فناوری از نامش قدیمی‌تر است.

3. **Industry reports in 2023 counted more than fifteen billion connected devices in the world, and the number is still growing.**
   گزارش‌های صنعت در سال ۲۰۲۳ شمار دستگاه‌های متصل جهان را بیش از ۱۵ میلیارد دانستند و این شمار همچنان رشد می‌کند.

4. **A device that sends data every second can use a hundred times more energy than one that sends every hour.**
   دستگاهی که هر ثانیه داده می‌فرستد می‌تواند صد برابر بیشتر از دستگاهی که هر ساعت می‌فرستد انرژی مصرف کند.

5. **MQTT was designed in 1999 for oil pipelines, where the connection was slow and the battery had to last for years.**
   پروتکل MQTT در سال ۱۹۹۹ برای خطوط لولهٔ نفت طراحی شد، جایی که اتصال کند بود و باتری باید سال‌ها دوام می‌آورد.

6. **A famous security study in 2023 found that many household devices still used a default password, and some of them were never updated.**
   پژوهشی مشهور امنیتی در سال ۲۰۲۳ نشان داد بسیاری از دستگاه‌های خانگی هنوز از رمز عبور پیش‌فرض استفاده می‌کنند و بعضی از آن‌ها هرگز به‌روزرسانی نشده‌اند.

7. **Interoperability is still the biggest problem: industry reports in 2023 showed that a typical smart home uses devices from several vendors and several apps.**
   هم‌کارکردی هنوز بزرگ‌ترین مشکل است: گزارش‌های صنعت در سال ۲۰۲۳ نشان داد یک خانهٔ هوشمند معمولی از دستگاه‌های چند فروشنده و چند اپلیکیشن استفاده می‌کند.

8. **The same idea is now used in farming: a soil sensor that reports every hour can save more water in one season than a new irrigation system.**
   همین ایده امروز در کشاورزی به کار می‌رود: حس‌گر خاکی که هر ساعت گزارش می‌دهد می‌تواند در یک فصل بیشتر از یک سامانهٔ نو آبیاری در آب صرفه‌جویی کند.
