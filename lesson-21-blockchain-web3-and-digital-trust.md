### 1. Lesson Objectives / اهداف درس

- ساختار یک دفتر کل توزیع‌شده را توضیح دهید و نقش درهم‌ساز، بلوک و ریشهٔ مرکل را بیان کنید.
- دو سازوکار اجماع (اثبات کار و اثبات سهام) را مقایسه کنید و تفاوت انشعاب سخت و نرم را بگویید.
- اجزای یک قرارداد هوشمند، توکن و کیف پول را با واژگان دقیق نام ببرید و کاربرد هرکدام را شرح دهید.
- ریسک‌های مالی و امنیتی این حوزه (نوسان، وثیقه، نقدکردن اجباری، پول‌شویی) را تحلیل کنید.
- تفاوت ردیابی‌پذیری و گمنامی را توضیح دهید و نقش اثبات بدون افشا را بیان کنید.
- با جمله‌واره‌های اسمی درست بنویسید و آن‌ها را در نقش فاعل، مفعول و متمم جمله به‌کار ببرید.

### 3. Reading / متن اصلی

**عنوان متن: Trust That Can Be Checked (اعتمادی که می‌توان بررسی کرد)**

**[1]** A blockchain is often described as a database that nobody owns, and that description is not wrong, but it hides the interesting part. What a blockchain really offers is a shared record that many strangers can update without asking one another for permission, and that can be checked afterwards by anyone. Mahsa, a graduate student, is building a provenance prototype for a herbal-products company, and Behrouz is an engineer at a payment company that is testing international settlement. They work on two different problems, and they ask the same question: what exactly is being trusted here?

**[2]** The foundation is a ledger, which is simply a list of entries, and a distributed ledger is the same list copied to many machines. Blocks group the entries, and the chain is what happens when each new block carries a fingerprint of the previous one. That fingerprint is produced by a cryptographic hash, a one-way function that turns any input into a fixed-length string, and a hash function with the same property is used everywhere in this field. A block normally contains a merkle tree, which lets a program prove that one entry belongs to the block without sending the whole block, and the merkle root is the single value that summarises the tree. Because a change in any entry changes the root, immutability is not a promise made by an administrator; it is a consequence of arithmetic.

**[3]** Agreement between machines is called consensus. In proof of work, mining is the work of finding a nonce that makes the block hash small enough, and the miner who succeeds first publishes the block. In proof of stake, the same job is given to a validator that has locked a large amount of the network's own currency, and a bad validator can lose it. Either way, the record starts with the genesis block, and each new block raises the block height by one. The whole structure is peer-to-peer: there is no master machine, and every node keeps its own copy.

**[4]** Nothing becomes final at once. A new block receives confirmations as later blocks are added on top of it, and finality is the point at which a reversal has become practically impossible. While a block waits, it sits in the mempool with every other unconfirmed transaction. Sometimes two miners publish a block at the same height, and this fork disappears after a few minutes, because one branch becomes longer. A hard fork is different: it changes the rules so deeply that old software can no longer join, and a soft fork tightens the rules in a way that older software still accepts.

**[5]** The classic attack on such a ledger is double spend, in which the same currency is used twice by publishing two conflicting histories, and it becomes realistic only for an actor who controls more than half of the network, which is why fifty-one percent attack is a phrase in every textbook. Other attacks need much less power. A sybil attack creates thousands of fake identities to influence a network, a replay attack copies a valid message from one chain to another, and a dust attack sends a tiny amount to an address in order to link the wallet to its owner. A vanity address is harmless in itself, but it is bought by people who want an address that looks familiar to somebody else.

**[6]** The programmable part of the field is the smart contract, where a program is published and then runs as written, without a second opinion from a server. A decentralized application is a normal user interface on top of such programs, and the user rarely knows which part is a contract and which part is a website. The security record of this software is mixed, and the reasons are known: a contract that can be upgraded through an upgradeable proxy gives the team a key to the money, and a contract that cannot be upgraded keeps its bugs for ever. Reentrancy is the classic bug, in which a contract calls another contract before it has finished its own bookkeeping, and a careful team answers with formal verification, a smart contract audit, and a code review before the first deposit arrives.

**[7]** Tokens are the second product of the same idea. A fungible token is interchangeable with its twins, while a non-fungible token represents one specific item, and this difference decides what can be done with it. New units are minted, unwanted ones are burned, and holders may lock their tokens in staking to earn a reward or to gain a vote. A governance token is often the same object with a political job attached to it, and this is where the technical and the social worlds meet.

**[8]** Keys are the whole of ownership. A wallet does not hold coins; it holds the private key that can sign for them, and the public key is the half that can be shared. Together they form a key pair, and the seed phrase is the human-readable form of everything the wallet knows: twelve words that rebuild the same key pair on another device. A hot wallet lives on a connected machine and is used daily; a cold wallet is kept offline; and in a custodial wallet the key is held by a company instead of by the user. Large amounts are protected by multisig, where several keys must agree, or by a threshold signature, which is the same idea in cryptographic form. If a single person must hold the only copy, social recovery distributes the ability to restore access among trusted friends.

**[9]** A chain cannot read the outside world by itself, so an oracle brings data in, and a price feed is the most common example. When the network is slow or expensive, the work moves to layer 2, and a rollup is the standard technique: thousands of transactions are compressed into one proof that is finally written to the main chain. A state channel is a more specialised answer, in which two parties exchange signed updates and only the opening and closing steps are published. Every operation costs gas, which is a fee for computation, and a transaction fee rises when the network is busy. An atomic swap exchanges two assets without a trusted middleman, and before anything is published on mainnet, it is tested on a testnet, where a faucet gives free currency to a new developer.

**[10]** Banks are interested for one practical reason: settlement. Banking has always been a chain of clearing, reconciliation, and double entry across an interbank network, and a cross-border payment or a remittance still takes days in many corridors. A blockchain-based system promises something that sounds simpler: programmable money that moves and settles in one step, with a micropayment becoming as cheap as any other amount.

**[11]** Money on a chain is a crowded field. A cryptocurrency is simply a token with its own network, an exchange is where it meets the ordinary currency, and a stablecoin tries to keep a peg to a national currency by holding a reserve. When that reserve is doubted, a depeg follows, and the whole market feels it, because volatility is not a technical detail but a risk that a user cannot manage. A speculative price is not a problem in itself, but a system that promises a fixed price and cannot prove its reserve is a problem of a different kind, and proof of reserves is the answer that the market now demands. Custody matters just as much: whoever holds the key holds the asset.

**[12]** Lending without a bank is built from collateral. A borrower locks an asset, takes a loan in another asset, and a liquidation happens automatically when the value of the locked asset falls below the agreed level. This machinery created liquidity in markets that had none, and it also created slippage, a difference between the expected and the received price that grows when the market is thin. A flash loan is a loan that is borrowed and repaid inside one transaction, which is useful for arbitrage and dangerous for a contract that has not been audited, because the borrower needs no collateral at all.

**[13]** Fraud is the shadow of every market. A rug pull is a project that collects deposits and then disappears, and an airdrop of free tokens is a common way to attract users before such an exit. Front-running is the practice of placing a trade just before a large known trade, and maximal extractable value is the total profit that is made by ordering transactions in the most profitable way. On the payment side, the serious problems are money laundering and the use of a mixer to hide the path between addresses, which is why know your customer checks, a whitelist of allowed counterparties, and anti-money laundering rules now sit at the door of almost every regulated exchange. A sanction or a legal order can also arrive from outside the network, and a compliant service must be able to answer it.

**[14]** Privacy is a design decision rather than a feature, and anonymity is the hardest version of it. A zero-knowledge proof lets a person prove a statement without revealing the data behind it: the witness is the hidden part, and a nullifier is the value that prevents the same secret from being used twice. Traceability is the opposite goal, and a blockchain is an excellent tracing machine, which is why chain analysis and taint analysis are used by investigators: with enough time, most addresses can be linked to an exchange and to a person.

**[15]** Digital identity uses the same machinery for a gentler purpose. A verifiable credential is issued by an issuer, held by a holder, and presented to a verifier without calling the issuer on every occasion. A self-sovereign identity gives the user the keys and the choice, a decentralized identifier gives each party a permanent name that its owner controls, and non-repudiation is what the signature provides: the holder cannot later deny the act. Notarization and timestamping of a document are the oldest examples, and the newer version adds attested data that machines can check. Authenticity, in this world, is not a claim in an email; it is a proof that can be verified by anyone.

**[16]** Governance is where the social part of the field becomes visible. A decentralized autonomous organization is a group with a treasury and a set of rules that live in code, and its decisions are made through a proposal, a ballot, and a voting period. A quorum decides how many votes are needed before a decision counts, and ratification turns a passed proposal into a rule. Some communities use a reputation score instead of money, and others use slashing, which takes part of a stake away from a member who breaks the rules; bonding and a security deposit serve the same purpose. When a real conflict appears, arbitration and a formal dispute resolution process are needed, because no program has ever settled a quarrel between people.

**[17]** Mahsa's project began with a simple requirement. The herbal-products company was being pressed by a buyer in Europe to prove that its raw material had been grown, tested, and transported under the declared conditions, and a folder of scanned certificates was no longer accepted. The question was whether a shared record could replace a pile of paper, and the answer depended on what could actually be written to the chain.

**[18]** Her first finding was that a blockchain stores very little. A batch number, a hash of a test report, a time, and a signature fit easily; a photograph, a laboratory file, or a farm's weather history does not. What she built was therefore a chain of hashes: the real documents stayed in a normal database, and the merkle root of each day's batch was written to the ledger. That the ledger is public does not mean that the data is public, and this sentence became the first line of her report.

**[19]** The second finding was harder and more useful. Whether a farm's declaration is true cannot be decided by a network; only a person who visits the farm can decide that. Her prototype therefore recorded not only the data but also the identity of the issuer and the exact time of the attestation, and it left the trust question in the open. What the buyer wanted was not a magical guarantee; it was a record that could be checked in ten minutes instead of three weeks, and that is what the pilot delivered.

**[20]** Behrouz arrived from the other direction. His company moves money between small exporters and their suppliers, and every transfer is a chain of paperwork: clearing, reconciliation, and a fee for each step. The pilot was narrow on purpose: one corridor, one stablecoin, and one rule that nothing could be settled until the compliance team had approved the counterparty. What he wanted to measure was the time to settlement, not the elegance of the design.

**[21]** The first incident was not a hack. The price feed that the settlement contract trusted had been read from one exchange, and during a quiet night that exchange printed a price that no other market agreed with. The contract behaved exactly as written: it paused, and every transfer in the corridor waited for four hours. Nobody lost money, which is the good news, and the pause was caused by a design choice that his own team had written, which is the lesson.

**[22]** The second incident was a depeg scare. A rumour about the reserve of a large stablecoin moved through the market in an hour, and for a short time every holder asked the same question: can the reserve be proved today? It could, and the peg held, but Behrouz wrote the experience into his report as a warning. The regulator's requirement, in his words, was not that the technology must be perfect; it was that the issuer must be able to show, at any moment, what backs the money.

**[23]** What a blockchain does well is narrow and real. It gives a shared history that many independent parties can read, it makes a silent change difficult to hide, and it lets a small program hold value and behave exactly as written. What it does not do is decide whether an entry is true, keep a secret, or repair a mistake that a person has already made.

**[24]** What surprises new users most is the absence of an undo button. A wrong transfer to a real address cannot be recalled, a lost key cannot be reset, and a contract that has been published cannot be edited in place. Whether this is a strength depends entirely on the task: a land registry wants a record that cannot be quietly changed, and a customer service desk wants the opposite.

**[25]** Three mistakes are repeated in almost every failed project. The first is putting private data on a public chain and calling it security. The second is promising a trustless system while a single administrator can upgrade the contract at midnight. The third is confusing a token with a business model: a project that issues a token and adds no customer has automated nothing except its own marketing.

**[26]** The cost question also deserves an honest answer. An older proof-of-work network consumes a large amount of electricity, and the newer designs consume much less, but no design is free. A rollup that runs on a single server is cheap and centralised; a network that is spread over thousands of machines is expensive and resilient. A team that cannot say which of the two it needs will pay for the wrong one.

**[27]** The newest chapter is the tokenized asset, where a real-world asset such as a building, an invoice, or a machine is represented by a token and traded in pieces. Fractional ownership of a small building becomes possible, and the settlement is cheap. The risk is old-fashioned: a token is only as good as the legal claim behind it, and the bridge between the token and the building is a contract, a custodian, and a lawyer.

**[28]** A land registry is the example that every ministry is shown, and it is a good test. The value is real, because a title that cannot be silently altered is worth a great deal in a country where paper can disappear. The difficulty is equally real, because the entry must be tied to a person, to a plot, and to the current law, and none of those three facts lives on the chain. The honest version of the project records the owner's claim and the surveyor's attestation, and it leaves the law to the court.

**[29]** A quieter use case is anti-counterfeit work. A tamper-evident seal with a serial number, a scan at the factory, and a hash written to a shared ledger make a counterfeit bottle much harder to pass, and the same record supports a digital twin of the product, so that a machine's service history travels with the machine. Supply chain provenance is the formal name for this work, and it succeeds where the incentive to lie is small and the incentive to check is large.

**[30]** Regulation has moved from the margin to the centre. A payment service that touches ordinary customers now runs know your customer and anti-money laundering checks, keeps a licence, and reports suspicious activity. Whether a token is a currency, a commodity, or a security is a question that every regulator answers differently, and a company that builds on the answer of one country must read the answer of the next one before it expands.

**[31]** So what should a student learn from this field? Learn the ledger, the key, and the contract before you learn the tokens, because the tokens change every season and the three foundations do not. Write down what the chain stores and what it does not. Then ask the only question that matters in a design review: who is trusted, for what, and how can that trust be checked?

**[32]** A useful prototype is also a measured one. Report the time to settlement, the cost of one transaction, the number of confirmations that your business actually needed, and the number of manual steps that disappeared. Then repeat the same measurement for the boring alternative, which is a shared database run by an operator. If the shared database wins, the shared database is the answer, and saying so is a professional decision rather than a failure.

**[33]** One last idea ties the whole chapter together. A distributed ledger is not a machine that removes trust; it is a machine that moves trust from a person to a record. Whether that move is an improvement depends on what the record contains, who can add to it, and who can read it. A system that answers those three questions honestly is a system worth building, and a system that avoids them is a marketing page with a logo.

۱. **جمله‌وارهٔ اسمی را با «که» یا بی‌واسطه برگردانید:** *What the buyer wanted was a record that could be checked* ← «آنچه خریدار می‌خواست، سندی بود که بتوان بررسی کرد». آوردن «چه چیزی خریدار می‌خواست بود» خطای ترتیب جمله است.
۲. **در ترجمهٔ *whether* از «آیا» استفاده نکنید:** *Whether the reserve exists cannot be known* ← «موجود بودن ذخیره را نمی‌توان دانست»، نه «آیا ذخیره وجود دارد را…». واژهٔ «آیا» فقط برای پرسش است.
۳. **فعل گزارشی + *that* را با «که» بیاورید:** *The report says that a price feed was used* ← «گزارش می‌گوید که از یک جریان قیمت استفاده شده است».
۴. **واژه‌های فنی را با معادل صنعتی ترجمه کنید:** *to settle a payment* ← «پرداخت را تسویه کردن»، *to mint* ← «ضرب کردن»، *to stake* ← «سهام‌سپاری کردن»، *to audit a contract* ← «قرارداد را حسابرسی کردن».
۵. **از ترجمهٔ فارسی‌نما بپرهیزید:** *trustless* ← «بدون نیاز به اعتماد» (نه «بی‌اعتماد»)، *permissionless* ← «بدون نیاز به مجوز» (نه «بی‌مجوز»).
۶. **سرواژه‌ها را یکدست نگه دارید:** KYC، AML، MEV و DAO را ترجمه نکنید؛ فقط در نخستین کاربرد معادل فارسی را در پرانتز بیاورید.
۷. **ترتیب صفت و اسم را رعایت کنید:** *a tokenized real-world asset* ← «دارایی واقعی توکنی‌شده»؛ اگر هر صفت را جدا ترجمه کنید، عبارت نامفهوم می‌شود.
۸. **از این ترجمه‌های تحت‌اللفظی بپرهیزید:** *dust attack* ← «حملهٔ گردوغبار» (نه «حملهٔ خاک»)، *airdrop* ← «ایردراپ/توزیع رایگان» (نه «پرت کردن هوا»)، *vanity address* ← «نشانی خودنمایانه» (نه «نشانی بیهوده»).

### 7. Grammar Focus / نکتهٔ گرامری

**موضوع: جمله‌واره‌های اسمی (noun clauses)**

**توضیح فارسی:**

جمله‌وارهٔ اسمی (*noun clause*) یک جملهٔ کامل است که نقش **اسم** در جملهٔ بزرگ‌تر را می‌گیرد؛ یعنی می‌تواند فاعل، مفعول، متمم یا بدل باشد. سه شکل اصلی دارد:

**۱) با *that*:** *That the ledger is public does not mean that the data is public.* این *that* فقط پیوند است و نقش دیگری ندارد؛ در گفتار معمولاً حذف می‌شود، ولی در نوشتار رسمی می‌ماند.

**۲) با *whether* (و *if*)**: برای انتخاب میان دو حالت به کار می‌رود: *Whether the reserve exists cannot be known from the chain.* نکتهٔ مهم: در نقش **فاعل** و بعد از حرف اضافه فقط *whether* می‌آید، نه *if*.

**۳) با کلمات پرسشی (*what, who, where, when, why, how*)**: *What the buyer wanted was a record that could be checked.* ترتیب جمله در این جمله‌واره **خبری** است، نه پرسشی.

کاربردهای آن را در جمله ببینید: فاعل (*What he said is true.*)، مفعول (*I know what the price is.*)، متمم فاعلی (*The problem is that nobody checked.*)، بعد از حرف اضافه (*It depends on whether the price is fresh.*) و در ساخت *It is important that ...* (*It is important that every issuer is named.*).

سه خطای رایج فارسی‌زبانان: ① نگه‌داشتن ترتیب پرسشی ✗ *I don't know what is the price* ← درست: *I don't know what the price is*؛ ② آوردن *if* در نقش فاعل ✗ *If he comes is unclear* ← درست: *Whether he comes is unclear*؛ ③ گذاشتن *that* در پرسش غیرمستقیم ✗ *He asked that where the ledger was* ← درست: *He asked where the ledger was*.

**جدول ۱ — سه شکل جمله‌وارهٔ اسمی:**

| شکل | نمونه | نکته |
|---|---|---|
| with *that* | That the peg held surprised nobody. | پیوند خنثی؛ در گفتار حذف می‌شود |
| with *whether* / *if* | I don't know whether the reserve exists. | در نقش فاعل فقط *whether* |
| with a question word | What the buyer wanted was a checked record. | ترتیب خبری، نه پرسشی |

**جدول ۲ — نقش جمله‌واره در جمله:**

| نقش | نمونه |
|---|---|
| فاعل | What he said is true. |
| مفعول | I know what the price is. |
| متمم فاعلی | The problem is that nobody checked. |
| بعد از حرف اضافه | It depends on whether the price is fresh. |
| با ساخت *It is + صفت + that* | It is important that every issuer is named. |
| بدل با *the fact that* | The fact that the ledger is public helps the buyer. |

**جدول ۳ — خطاهای رایج و شکل درست:**

| خطا | شکل درست |
|---|---|
| ✗ I don't know what is the price. | I don't know what the price is. |
| ✗ If the reserve exists is unknown. | Whether the reserve exists is unknown. |
| ✗ He asked that where the ledger was. | He asked where the ledger was. |
| ✗ What he said it was true. | What he said was true. |

### 9. Writing Task / تمرین نگارش

**موضوع: A Record I Had to Prove — سندی که مجبور شدم اثبات کنم**

یک سند واقعی را انتخاب کنید که **خودتان** مجبور شده‌اید آن را نشان دهید یا ثابت کنید: کارنامه، گواهی مهارت، مدرک پایان دوره، فاکتور خرید، یا اجازه‌نامهٔ کارگاه. سپس دربارهٔ آن بنویسید:

- چه کسی آن سند را می‌خواست و برای چه کاری؟
- چه چیزی در آن سند قابل بررسی بود و چه چیزی فقط ادعا بود؟
- اگر آن سند به‌صورت اعتبارنامهٔ قابل‌بازبینی صادر می‌شد، چه چیزی عوض می‌شد؟
- چه چیزی **عوض نمی‌شد**، حتی با وجود فناوری بهتر؟ (به یاد داشته باشید که شبکه نمی‌تواند تشخیص دهد ادعا راست است)
- یک جملهٔ محتاطانه بنویسید که محدودیت پیشنهاد خودتان را بپذیرد.

**شرایط نگارش:**

- نوشته بین **۲۰۰ تا ۳۰۰ واژه** باشد.
- دست‌کم **۵ جمله‌وارهٔ اسمی** داشته باشد؛ در میان آن‌ها یکی با *that* در نقش فاعل، یکی با *whether* و یکی با کلمهٔ پرسشی بیاید.
- یک جمله با *the fact that* در متن باشد.
- دست‌کم **۲ اصطلاح ربطی** از این فهرست به‌کار ببرید: *however*, *therefore*, *even so*, *as a result*.
- دست‌کم **۸ واژه** از جدول‌های واژگان بخش ۲ استفاده شود.

### 10. Mini Project / پروژهٔ کوچک

**انشا (نیمه‌تحقیقی): یک زنجیرهٔ اعتماد برای یک سند واقعی**

سندی را انتخاب کنید که **خودتان** در زندگی روزمره با آن روبه‌رو می‌شوید (گواهی مهارت، سند اجاره، فاکتور خرید تجهیزات، یا مجوز یک کارگاه) و طرح یک زنجیرهٔ اعتماد کوچک برای آن بنویسید:

- **سند و صادرکننده:** چه کسی سند را صادر می‌کند و چه کسی آن را بررسی می‌کند؟
- **شکل دستی:** مسیر سند را روی کاغذ بکشید: صادرکننده ← امضا ← درهم‌ساز ← ثبت در دفتر کل ← دارنده ← بازبین. روی هر پیکان بنویسید چه داده‌ای منتقل می‌شود.
- **جدول دستی مقایسه:** جدولی با چهار سطر بکشید و سند کاغذی را با اعتبارنامهٔ قابل‌بازبینی مقایسه کنید: هزینهٔ صدور، سرعت بررسی، امکان جعل، وابستگی به یک سازمان.
- **بیرون از زنجیره:** سه داده‌ای را نام ببرید که نباید در دفتر کل عمومی نوشته شوند و دلیل هر کدام را بگویید.
- **مرز اعتماد:** اگر صادرکننده دروغ بگوید، کدام بخش سامانه او را متوقف می‌کند و کدام بخش نمی‌تواند؟
- **جمله‌های گرامری:** پنج جمله‌وارهٔ اسمی بنویسید، شامل یک *whether* و یک کلمهٔ پرسشی.

**شرایط نگارش:**

- گزارش را **دست‌نویس روی کاغذ** بنویسید و در **جلسهٔ بعد** به مدرس تحویل دهید.
- دست‌کم **۲۵۰ واژه** باشد.
- دست‌کم **۵ جمله‌وارهٔ اسمی** و **۲ جمله** با *might* یا *could* (لحن محتاطانه) داشته باشد.
- طرح دستی مسیر سند و جدول دستی مقایسه در گزارش باشد.
- دست‌کم **۱۲ واژه** از جدول‌های واژگان بخش ۲ در گزارش به‌کار رفته باشد.

**واژه‌های یاری‌دهنده:** distributed ledger · merkle root · cryptographic hash · timestamp · verifiable credential · issuer · holder · non-repudiation · attestation · traceability · zero-knowledge proof · proof of reserves

### 11. Did You Know? / آیا می‌دانستید؟

1. **The design paper behind the first blockchain appeared in late 2008, and the first block of that network was created in January 2009.**
   مقالهٔ طراحی نخستین زنجیرهٔ بلوکی در اواخر ۲۰۰۸ منتشر شد و نخستین بلوک آن شبکه در ژانویهٔ ۲۰۰۹ ساخته شد.

2. **The term "Web3" was used in 2014 by a co-founder of one of the first smart-contract platforms, long before it became a marketing word.**
   اصطلاح «وب ۳» در سال ۲۰۱۴ توسط یکی از هم‌بنیان‌گذاران نخستین پلتفرم‌های قرارداد هوشمند به کار رفت؛ مدتی طولانی پیش از آنکه به واژه‌ای تبلیغاتی تبدیل شود.

3. **In 2016, an attack emptied about 3.6 million units from the smart contract of a large fund, and the community answered with a hard fork.**
   در سال ۲۰۱۶ حمله‌ای حدود ۳٫۶ میلیون واحد را از قرارداد هوشمند یک صندوق بزرگ بیرون کشید و جامعه با یک انشعاب سخت پاسخ داد.

4. **In 2022, the second-largest network changed from proof of work to proof of stake, and the foundation behind it reported that its energy use had fallen by more than 99 percent.**
   در سال ۲۰۲۲ دومین شبکهٔ بزرگ از اثبات کار به اثبات سهام تغییر کرد و بنیاد پشت آن گزارش داد که مصرف انرژی‌اش بیش از ۹۹ درصد کاهش یافته است.

5. **Zero-knowledge proofs were described in 1985 by three researchers, and they became practical for blockchains only after 2016.**
   اثبات‌های بدون افشا در سال ۱۹۸۵ توسط سه پژوهشگر معرفی شدند و تنها پس از ۲۰۱۶ برای زنجیره‌های بلوکی کاربردی شدند.

6. **In 2021, a country in Central America made a cryptocurrency legal tender, and international organisations warned about the risks of that decision.**
   در سال ۲۰۲۱ کشوری در آمریکای مرکزی یک رمزارز را پول قانونی اعلام کرد و سازمان‌های بین‌المللی دربارهٔ خطرهای آن تصمیم هشدار دادند.

7. **Surveys published in 2023 reported that the large majority of central banks were studying or piloting a digital version of their own currency.**
   بررسی‌های منتشرشده در ۲۰۲۳ گزارش کردند که اکثریت بزرگ بانک‌های مرکزی در حال بررسی یا آزمون نسخهٔ دیجیتال پول خود بودند.

8. **Land-registry pilots have been run in several countries since 2016, and most of them stopped at the pilot stage: the technical part worked, and the legal part did not.**
   آزمایش‌های ثبت اسناد مالکیت از سال ۲۰۱۶ در چند کشور اجرا شده است و بیشتر آن‌ها در مرحلهٔ آزمایش ماندند: بخش فنی کار کرد و بخش حقوقی نه.
